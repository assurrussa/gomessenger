# v0.2.2 release-candidate: batch-16 Outbox pool A/B

> Historical tuning baseline. The later
> [clean interleaved batch matrix](v0.2.2-clean-batch-matrix.md) uses the
> selected `8 + 2` pool layout without independently comparing it with `9 + 1`,
> and does not reproduce this series' batch-16 1500 msg/s result: batch 16
> passes 1/3 there, while batch 100 passes 3/3. Use the later matrix for
> release-facing batch and capacity claims.

## Technical summary

The durable PostgreSQL → Outbox → NATS JetStream → Inbox → PostgreSQL pipeline
preserved integrity in all 18 measured runs: 3,235,264 accepted business effects
reconciled to the same number of projections after drain, with zero duplicate
effects, broker redeliveries, DLQ messages, or HTTP failures.

The strongest performance result is narrower: with reservation batch `16`,
two Outbox workers, two NATS consumers, and a ten-connection Outbox pool split
as `8 producer + 2 relay`, the 120-second measured stage sustained 1500 msg/s
in 3/3 clean runs. The `9 producer + 1 relay` split failed the latency
criterion in both clean 1500 msg/s runs. Neither split sustained 2000 msg/s.

These are checkout-local release-candidate results, not production-readiness
evidence. The runs identify tested commit
`e45d1041527dea2548696a87096f6f6c1da03127`; the later release-candidate head
`6836d1b304a10026c16be4062280f19a9ef9db8f` changed only `CHANGELOG.md` and
`implementation-notes.md`. No `v0.2.2` tag existed when this analysis was
created.

## Results

Medians use clean runs only. “Pass” is the report's complete sustainability
decision, not merely end-of-run reconciliation.

| 9 + 1      |   1000 |        3/3 |     999.858 |        999.858 |     79.523 ms |         17 |            0 |  0.548 s |
| 8 + 2      |   1000 |        2/3 |     999.692 |        999.692 |     79.179 ms |         16 |            0 |  0.644 s |

At 1500 msg/s the second relay connection removes the observed relay-pool
starvation: the `8 + 2` series remains below the configured 2-second business
p95 limit in every repetition, while the two clean `9 + 1` runs reach about
17 seconds despite ending near the target throughput. End-rate throughput alone
would therefore conceal severe queueing.

Measured-stage counter deltas in `samples.jsonl` support that diagnosis. The
clean median relay-connection acquisition wait is about 73.0 seconds with
159,765.5 empty-acquire observations for `9 + 1`, versus 0.366 seconds and
8,723 observations for `8 + 2`. These are pool counter deltas, not end-to-end
latency measurements.

At 2000 msg/s, `8 + 2` improves the clean median relay rate by 6.4%, consumer
rate by 7.4%, p95 by 22.1%, Outbox lag by 55.3%, consumer lag by 61.1%, and
drain time by 20.8% relative to `9 + 1`. Both configurations still fail, so
those figures describe reduced degradation, not supported capacity.

## What this proves

- Every accepted message in every run reached exactly one committed projection
  by the post-drain reconciliation boundary.
- The checkout-local pipeline remained internally consistent while accumulating
  and draining backlog, including all six failed 2000 msg/s runs.
- Under the recorded single-node topology, the `8 + 2` pool split is the only
  tested configuration with a clean 3/3 sustainability result at 1500 msg/s.

The result does not change GoMessenger's at-least-once public contract. It does
not prove exactly-once external effects, multi-node behavior, failure recovery,
long-duration stability, or production readiness.

## Scope and methodology

- 18 runs: two pool layouts × three target rates × three repetitions.
- 30-second warm-up followed by a 120-second measured open-loop stage.
- Small payload profile; PostgreSQL 17.9; NATS 2.12.3; Outbox v0.12.0; Go 1.27.
- Linux/arm64 containers with 12 logical CPUs on a Darwin/arm64 host.
- Two Outbox workers, reservation batch 16, two NATS consumers, and ten Outbox
  PostgreSQL connections split between producer and relay.
- Sustainability requires the requested rate, no dropped iterations or HTTP
  failures, the configured latency/backlog conditions, completed measured
  drain, and post-drain integrity.

PostgreSQL telemetry points to WAL and database contention rather than exhausted
container memory. WAL volume rises from about 0.89 GB at 1000 msg/s to
1.92–1.97 GB at 2000 msg/s. At 2000 msg/s the backlog and drain expansion remain
material in both layouts, so adding another relay connection only moves the
bottleneck boundary.

## Data quality and limitations

- `producer9-relay1-1500-r1` records `gitDirty=true`. It remains in the
  integrity totals and audit trail but is excluded from clean medians and clean
  pass counts.
- `producer8-relay2-1000-r3` dropped 181 of 120,001 offered iterations. All
  119,820 accepted messages reconciled, but the run correctly fails the
  zero-drop sustainability contract. This non-monotonic outlier should be
  rerun before calling 1000 msg/s a repeatable `8 + 2` floor.
- Warm-up drain exceeded its 30-second performance limit in every 1500 and 2000
  msg/s run. The measured stage started only after full quiescence, so backlog
  did not carry into the measurement; the series still has a cold-start and
  local-I/O reproducibility caveat.
- All runs use batch 16. There is no matched batch-1 control, so this dataset
  cannot support a “batch 16 is faster” claim or a default change.
- Three repetitions establish a useful baseline, not a confidence interval.
  The tests are short and share one workstation.

## Release use

Keep this dataset as historical integrity and pool-tuning evidence only. It
records exact reconciliation of 3,055,263 accepted effects across 17 clean
local runs and explains why the later matrix selected the `8 + 2` pool split,
but its batch-16 1500 msg/s result was not reproduced and must not appear as a
`v0.2.2` capacity claim.

Use the release-note wording and batch-100 result from the
[clean interleaved batch matrix](v0.2.2-clean-batch-matrix.md#release-use-and-recommendation).
