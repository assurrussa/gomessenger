# v0.2.2 release-candidate: clean reservation-batch matrix

## Technical summary

The clean, interleaved matrix preserves the strongest correctness result: all
2,213,287 accepted effects across 18 runs reconciled to exactly the same number
of committed projections after drain. There were no HTTP failures, broker
redeliveries, duplicate Inbox effects, or DLQ messages.

The performance result changes the release-facing recommendation. With two
Outbox workers, two NATS consumers, and the ten-connection Outbox pool split as
`8 producer + 2 relay`, reservation batch `100` completed the 1500 msg/s cell
in 3/3 clean runs. Its business p95 ranged from 114.589 to 232.524 ms. Batch
`16` completed the same cell in only 1/3 runs; the other repetitions reached
4,843.064 and 6,039.017 ms p95.

This supports batch `100` for the recorded benchmark profile at 1500 msg/s. It
does not establish a universal library default or a production capacity claim:
one 650 msg/s run and two 1000 msg/s runs dropped scheduled k6 iterations, and
all measurements are short checkout-local runs on one workstation.

The tested source is exact clean commit
`6836d1b304a10026c16be4062280f19a9ef9db8f`. No `v0.2.2` tag existed when the
matrix was analysed.

## Batch 100 is the repeatable 1500 msg/s cell

Medians include all three clean runs in each cell. “Pass” is the complete
sustainability verdict: requested open-loop rate, zero load-generator drops and
HTTP failures, latency/backlog criteria, completed drain, and post-drain
integrity.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 500 | 3/3 | 499.683 | 499.683 | 97.012 ms | 38 | 0 | 52 | 0.591 s |
| 1 | 650 | 2/3 | 649.542 | 649.542 | 98.538 ms | 42 | 0 | 136 | 0.516 s |
| 16 | 1000 | 2/3 | 999.633 | 999.633 | 82.564 ms | 7 | 0 | 185 | 0.637 s |
| 16 | 1500 | 1/3 | 1499.967 | 1499.942 | 4,843.064 ms | 5 | 4 | 9,477 | 0.724 s |
| 100 | 1000 | 2/3 | 999.683 | 999.417 | 90.402 ms | 36 | 0 | 102 | 0.577 s |
| 100 | 1500 | 3/3 | 1499.567 | 1499.567 | 182.564 ms | 45 | 0 | 781 | 0.756 s |

At 1500 msg/s, batch `100` reduces the observed median business p95 by 96.2%
and peak business backlog by 91.8% relative to batch `16`. End-of-stage
throughput and lag alone conceal the batch-16 queueing: both batches finish
near 1500 msg/s and batch 16 even has the lower final Outbox lag, while its
peak backlog and end-to-end latency are much worse.

The PostgreSQL claim telemetry is directionally consistent with improved
reservation efficiency. Batch `100` makes 11.8% fewer median claim calls,
returns 13.3% more rows per call, and spends 53.2% less median aggregate claim
execution time than batch `16` at 1500 msg/s. These are descriptive counters,
not proof that reservation size is the only cause.

## Batch 1 establishes 500 msg/s, not a need for a 300 msg/s run

Batch `1` passes 500 msg/s in 3/3 repetitions with business p95 tightly bounded
between 96.677 and 97.827 ms. A 300 msg/s run would therefore add no useful
release boundary evidence for this profile.

At 650 msg/s, two runs pass with p95 below 99 ms. The third retains low latency
but drops 286 scheduled iterations and therefore correctly fails the strict
zero-drop contract. The measured data support a repeatable floor of 500 msg/s
for batch `1`; they do not support 650 msg/s as a 3/3 floor.

## Integrity remains exact for every accepted effect

Across the matrix, k6 offered 2,214,016 iterations and the application accepted
2,213,287. All accepted orders, measurements, broker confirmations, and
projections reconcile after drain. The difference is exactly the 729 scheduled
k6 iterations dropped before HTTP acceptance; it is not lost application data.

The aggregate dropped-iteration rate is 0.0329%. There are zero HTTP failures,
redeliveries, duplicate effects, invalid measurements, missing links, and DLQ
messages. This is strong pipeline-integrity evidence, but it does not change
GoMessenger's at-least-once contract or prove exactly-once external effects.

## Scope, definitions, and method

- 18 clean runs at report specification `1.3`: six batch/rate cells with three
  repetitions per cell.
- Balanced interleaving: every cell appears once per repetition, with the order
  reversed and mixed across repetitions to reduce fixed-order bias.
- 30-second warm-up followed by one 120-second measured open-loop stage.
- Small payload profile; PostgreSQL 17.9; NATS 2.12.3; Outbox v0.12.0; Go 1.27.
- Linux/arm64 containers with 12 logical CPUs on a Darwin/arm64 host.
- Two Outbox workers, two NATS consumers, reservation batch `1`, `16`, or
  `100`, and ten Outbox PostgreSQL connections split as `8 + 2`.
- Business p95 measures accepted HTTP work through the committed projection.
  End Outbox lag is `staged - published`; end consumer lag is
  `published - committed`. Peak business backlog is the largest corresponding
  gap observed during the measured stage.
- A run is sustainable only when the target rate, zero-drop and zero-HTTP-error
  load contract, configured latency/backlog limits, measured drain, and
  post-drain reconciliation all pass.

The invalid long-ID preflight created 18 directories before this matrix but
never entered warm-up and produced no `report.json` or measured k6 stage. Those
directories are excluded from the dataset and raw evidence archive.

## Limitations and robustness checks

- Batch `16` at 1000, batch `100` at 1000, and batch `1` at 650 each have one
  k6 scheduling failure. The failed runs dropped 218, 225, and 286 iterations
  respectively while every accepted effect reconciled. Their aggregate
  producer-pool acquisition wait also spikes relative to the two passing
  repetitions, which is consistent with transient host or producer-side
  contention but does not identify a single cause.
- The two batch-16 failures at 1500 are different: k6 drops are zero, but
  business p95 exceeds the 2-second SLO and peak backlog reaches 9,477 and
  10,978. This is an application-pipeline capacity result, not merely a load
  generator verdict.
- Warm-up drain at 1500 takes 34.99–36.23 seconds in all six runs, above the
  configured 30-second performance limit. Every measured stage starts only
  after full quiescence, so backlog does not carry into the denominator; the
  result still has cold-start and local-I/O variability.
- Three 120-second repetitions establish a release baseline, not a confidence
  interval, soak result, restart/failure result, multi-node result, or
  production-readiness result.
- The earlier pool A/B series ran in fixed blocks and reported batch `16` at
  1500 as 3/3. This clean interleaved matrix reports 1/3, so the earlier result
  remains useful for choosing `8 + 2` over `9 + 1` but is superseded for
  release-facing batch-16 capacity claims.

## Release use and recommendation

Release notes may state that exact clean commit `6836d1b3` reconciled 2.213
million accepted effects across 18 checkout-local capacity runs. For the
batch-100 `8 + 2` small-payload profile, all three 120-second measured stages
at 1500 msg/s were sustainable: median relay and consumer throughput were both
1499.567 msg/s, median business p95 was 182.564 ms, median end Outbox and
consumer lag were 45 and 0, median peak business backlog was 781, and median
drain was 0.756 seconds. All three runs had zero dropped iterations, HTTP
failures, redeliveries, duplicate effects, and DLQ messages, with exact
post-drain reconciliation.

Do not describe 1500 msg/s as production capacity, generalize batch `100` to
all payloads and database hosts, or claim that the 1000 msg/s cells pass 3/3.
Keep the public reservation-batch default unchanged until a representative
pilot or broader workload matrix validates the trade-off. For the documented
single-node capacity profile, use batch `100` as the preferred tuning value.

## Recommended next checks

1. Repeat batch `100` at 1500 msg/s from the exact immutable release tag, then
   run a 30–60 minute soak with the same profile.
2. Isolate or reserve load-generator CPU before repeating the three cells with
   k6 scheduling drops; retain zero drops as the release-evidence rule.
3. Exercise restart, redelivery, and PostgreSQL checkpoint pressure before
   turning this checkout-local result into an operational capacity statement.
4. Use the real-service pilot in ADR-0002 as the production-readiness gate.
